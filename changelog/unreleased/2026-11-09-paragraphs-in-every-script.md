- **Each paragraph of a text reads in its own direction, and an edit reshapes only the paragraphs it touches in every script.** A `\n` ends a paragraph for the bidirectional algorithm, and each paragraph takes its direction from its first strong character, left to right when it has none, as a natural writing direction does: a Hebrew or Arabic line in an English note reads right to left and, start-aligned, aligns right, with its caret, selection and hits following it. Before, every paragraph took the direction of the text's first strong character, and a neutral at a paragraph's edge took the direction of the paragraphs around it. Spaces, digits and punctuation starting a paragraph take its own script, so they can kern with the text after them. Typing in a long note in Japanese, or holding Arabic or Hebrew, no longer reshapes the whole note (a 50 KB note: about 12 and 23 ms a keystroke to 0.25 ms). Unwrapped text with right-to-left letters still lays out as one layout in one direction.
